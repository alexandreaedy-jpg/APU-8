# APU-8 Light / Deluxe Product Brief

## Purpose

Define a realistic two-product strategy for commercializing `APU-8` without forcing a single over-ambitious first product.

This brief assumes:

- the core NES audio/control architecture is validated
- the product line is built around the same overall concept
- the market is niche, premium, and musician/retro oriented

## Core Strategy

The line should not be split into:

- "cheap bad version"
- "real version"

It should be split into:

- **Light** = compact, accessible, controller-like module
- **Deluxe** = full instrument-style module

These are two different promises:

- Light = easy entry, compact, lower price, still powerful through MIDI CC
- Deluxe = maximum immediacy, maximum hands-on control, premium object

## APU-8 Light

### Product idea

A compact expansion module inspired by the scale and clarity of a Nintendo accessory.

It should feel:

- clean
- small
- obvious
- desirable
- not intimidating

### User promise

"I can plug this into a NES setup and get a usable, expressive chiptune sound module without buying an oversized control surface."

### Physical direction

- small case
- controller-like footprint
- minimal front panel
- no sliders
- only essential buttons/selectors

### Suggested front-panel controls

- voice selector
- a few essential function buttons
- maybe one or two mode selectors
- maybe one compact encoder / knob only if really justified

### What is intentionally not on the front

- full ADSR surface
- detailed modulation controls
- arp deep controls
- screen-heavy workflow

### Interaction model

The Light version relies on:

- a few direct front-panel essentials
- full MIDI CC support for deeper control

It should be strong for:

- DAW users
- MIDI controller users
- people who want the sound and concept first

### Target customer

- chiptune-curious musicians
- synth users who already work with MIDI CC
- retro gamers who want a real music-capable NES accessory
- collectors who want the most affordable serious model

### Main strengths

- lower cost of entry
- easier to explain
- easier to manufacture
- cleaner industrial design problem
- less risky first commercial product

### Main risks

- can feel "too dependent on external MIDI CC" if not positioned well
- may disappoint users who expect full panel immediacy

### Target price

- ideal zone: **`199 €` to `249 €`**
- hard ceiling goal: **below `250 €`**

### Production difficulty

- **moderate**

This should be the easier version to launch first.

## APU-8 Deluxe

### Product idea

A full-featured premium control surface built around the NES sound engine.

It should feel:

- expressive
- instrument-like
- serious
- premium
- performance-oriented

### User promise

"I get a true dedicated NES instrument with as much direct control as possible on the front panel."

### Physical direction

- larger case
- richer front panel
- more obvious instrument identity
- more controls visible at once
- more premium presentation

### Suggested front-panel controls

- ADSR controls
- modulation controls
- arp controls
- voice selectors
- modulation selector
- switches / buttons
- screen if useful and stable

### Interaction model

The Deluxe version is built around:

- direct front-panel access
- reduced dependence on external CC mapping
- more self-contained workflow

It should be strong for:

- hardware musicians
- live performers
- power users
- collectors who want the flagship version

### Target customer

- dedicated chiptune musicians
- synth hardware enthusiasts
- boutique instrument buyers
- collectors willing to pay for a standout premium object

### Main strengths

- strongest differentiation
- strongest "instrument" identity
- highest emotional appeal
- most impressive campaign/demo object

### Main risks

- more expensive to build
- more complex to assemble
- more support burden
- more opportunity for feature creep
- more dangerous as a first product

### Target price

- realistic zone: **`349 €` to `449 €`**
- likely sweet spot: **`379 €` to `399 €`**

### Production difficulty

- **high**

## Shared Foundation

Whenever possible, both products should reuse:

- the same core sound/control architecture
- the same main firmware family
- the same cartridge-side logic direction
- shared internal subsystems where practical

But they do **not** need to share:

- the same enclosure
- the same front panel
- the same number of physical controls

## Strategic Recommendation

### Best launch order

1. **Launch Light first**
2. **Use Deluxe as later premium evolution**, or at most as a carefully staged premium tier once the Light is stable

### Why Light should lead

- lower price barrier
- broader addressable niche
- simpler manufacturing
- simpler message
- lower support risk
- better first Kickstarter candidate

### Why Deluxe should not lead unless absolutely ready

- higher development burden
- higher unit cost
- higher expectation level
- higher risk of slipping into feature overload

## Commercial Role of Each Product

### Light

Role:

- market entry product
- lower-friction Kickstarter offer
- main sales volume candidate

### Deluxe

Role:

- halo product
- aspirational version
- premium upsell
- stronger image builder

## Messaging Difference

### Light positioning sentence

"A compact NES sound control module with the essentials on the surface and full MIDI depth behind it."

### Deluxe positioning sentence

"A premium dedicated NES instrument with hands-on control across the full sound engine."

## Risk Summary

### Light risk level

- **manageable**

### Deluxe risk level

- **significant**

### Combined simultaneous launch risk

- **very high** if both products require substantially different hardware/mechanical solutions at the same time

## Final Recommendation

The strongest product strategy is:

- **Light as the first serious commercial target**
- **Deluxe as the flagship evolution**

This gives `APU-8`:

- a more realistic entry point
- a clearer Kickstarter story
- a broader buyer funnel
- and still preserves the long-term dream of a full premium instrument

In short:

- **Light sells the concept**
- **Deluxe fulfills the dream**
