# APU-8 V2.5 Kickstarter Launch Plan

## Goal

Define the most realistic first commercial launch strategy for a finished `APU-8 V2.5`:

- dedicated NES cartridge
- external expansion-port control module
- dedicated hands-on controls
- premium but still realistic niche-musician pricing

This plan assumes:

- the product is technically validated
- shipping is charged separately
- VAT/taxes are handled separately
- the first run is small-batch boutique hardware

## Product Positioning

The product is **not** just "MIDI on NES".

Its commercial value is:

- dedicated tactile controls
- immediate synth-like workflow
- stable timing and low-latency note control
- premium object for musicians and chiptune users
- console-matched industrial design

Target public price:

- realistic range: `279 €` to `349 €`
- best center price: `299 €`

## Base Product Scope

Include in the first launch:

- cartridge with production ROM/runtime
- expansion-port module
- ADSR controls
- modulation controls
- arp speed + sync
- voice selector
- modulation selector
- MIDI input
- finished enclosure

Do **not** include in the base campaign unless already stable:

- LCD / OLED screen
- too many premium mechanical features
- advanced secondary features that complicate firmware or assembly

Those can be stretch goals or later revisions.

## Financial Assumptions

### Kickstarter fees

Kickstarter applies:

- `5%` platform fee
- roughly `3% to 5%` payment processing

For planning, use **`9%` total platform/payment overhead**.

### One-time preproduction costs

Reasonable planning block for first launch:

- expansion-port connector development/tooling
- enclosure/tooling/proto iterations
- electrical validation and final prototypes
- campaign assets/video/samples
- test fixtures / jigs / QC prep

Estimated one-time block:

- **`35 000 €`**

### Loaded unit cost assumptions

These are planning estimates for:

- electronics
- PCB assembly
- controls
- connector/cable
- enclosure
- packaging
- test/QC
- assembly overhead

Estimated loaded COGS:

- `250 units` -> **`105 € / unit`**
- `350 units` -> **`92 € / unit`**
- `500 units` -> **`82 € / unit`**

## Revised 350-Unit Scenario With Custom Expansion Plug Cost Added

This revised model makes the custom expansion plug explicit as a separate cost line.

### Working assumption for the plug

Use this as the business-plan planning figure:

- **custom expansion plug effective cost: `25 € / unit` at 350 units**

This is a blended planning number intended to cover:

- molded connector body
- contacts
- connector-side assembly
- connector-related amortization and inefficiency

### Revised loaded unit cost at 350 units

Previous loaded unit cost assumption:

- base product: **`92 € / unit`**

Add explicit custom expansion plug:

- expansion plug: **`25 € / unit`**

Revised loaded unit cost:

- **`117 € / unit`**

### Revised 350-unit totals

- units: `350`
- average pledge: `300 €`
- gross campaign: `105 000 €`
- net after 9% platform/payment fees: **`95 550 €`**
- revised loaded COGS: `350 x 117 €` = **`40 950 €`**
- one-time preproduction costs: **`35 000 €`**

Remaining buffer:

- **`19 600 €`**

### Interpretation

This is still viable.

But it is no longer a "comfortable by default" scenario.

It becomes:

- workable if the campaign is well prepared
- healthy if shipping and VAT stay outside the pledge
- risky if scope grows too much

### Minimum / Healthy / Comfortable Targets

For the `350-unit` plan with a `25 €` custom plug:

- **minimum viable target:** `95 000 €`
- **healthy target:** `105 000 €`
- **comfortable target:** `110 000 €`

At those levels:

| Campaign target | Net after 9% fees | Remaining after `40 950 €` COGS and `35 000 €` one-time costs |
|---|---:|---:|
| `95 000 €` | `86 450 €` | `10 500 €` |
| `105 000 €` | `95 550 €` | `19 600 €` |
| `110 000 €` | `100 100 €` | `24 150 €` |

### Updated Recommendation

If the custom expansion plug is budgeted at **`25 € / unit`**:

- keep `350 units` as the launch sweet spot
- move the real funding target from `95 000 €` toward **`105 000 €`**
- keep `299 €` as the core price anchor
- avoid adding LCD or feature creep to the base offering

### Practical Reading

With the plug explicitly included:

- **`95k€`** = possible, but tight
- **`105k€`** = realistic and sane
- **`110k€`** = much safer first campaign

## Recommendation

### Best launch strategy

Launch with:

- **`350 units`**
- **campaign goal: `95 000 €`**
- **standard pledge: `299 €`**

This is the best balance between:

- realistic funding target
- manageable production size
- healthy buffer
- lower market risk than `500` units
- much better viability than `250` units

### Suggested pledge structure

- `100` Early Bird units at **`279 €`**
- `200` Standard units at **`299 €`**
- `50` Founder / Supporter units at **`349 €`**

That mix lands close to a `300 €` average pledge.

## Why 350 Units Is the Sweet Spot

### 250 units

Pros:

- easier to sell out
- lower manufacturing commitment

Cons:

- too little safety margin
- almost no room for mistakes, rework, or warranty issues
- dangerous if connector or enclosure costs drift upward

### 350 units

Pros:

- still believable for a niche launch
- enough volume to improve COGS
- enough buffer to survive inevitable surprises
- strong balance of scarcity and viability

Cons:

- requires a credible campaign and polished prototype

### 500 units

Pros:

- best unit economics
- strongest long-term gross margin

Cons:

- much higher demand risk
- more cash tied up
- harder first campaign target psychologically

## Product Strategy Notes

### What makes the product commercially strong

- dedicated controls
- better workflow than DAW-only solutions
- stronger identity than "just another MIDI bridge"
- visual and tactile identity close to the NES ecosystem

### Biggest business risk

The main industrial risk is **not** the synth concept itself.

It is:

- the custom expansion-port connector
- the mechanical finish level expected by buyers

That means the first commercial version should prioritize:

- robust connector strategy
- simple, clean enclosure
- reliable controls
- stable firmware

before premium feature creep.

## Practical Go / No-Go Rule

Proceed to Kickstarter only if all of the following are true:

- `V2.5` timing and stability are proven
- at least one convincing video demo exists
- the connector solution is manufacturable at scale
- enclosure strategy is costed
- unit assembly/QC workflow is repeatable

If one of those is still unclear, stay in prototype mode.

## Final Recommendation

If `APU-8 V2.5` is musically strong and mechanically convincing:

- **launch 350 units**
- **ask for about `95 000 €`**
- **price the main pledge at `299 €`**
- **keep shipping and taxes outside the pledge price**
- **leave LCD/screen features out of the base offering**

That is the best first-launch balance between:

- credibility
- manufacturability
- margin
- and survivable risk.
